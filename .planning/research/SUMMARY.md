# Project Research Summary

**Project:** JAFN — "Ask about your pay" (working codename; brand and domain are a Phase 0 decision)
**Domain:** Static, client-side, privacy-first EU regulatory web toolkit + open-source TypeScript compliance engine, over community-maintained legal data for 27 jurisdictions
**Researched:** 2026-09-10
**Confidence:** MEDIUM overall — HIGH on stack and architecture, LOW-to-MEDIUM on the legal substrate the product's value rests on

## Executive Summary

This is a legal-information product wearing a calculator's clothes, and every researcher independently concluded that its hard problems are not engineering problems. The stack is settled and boring in the good sense: a fully prerendered Astro 7.3.2 site on Cloudflare, React islands for the four modules, a zero-runtime-dependency MIT engine published to npm, and a monorepo whose licence boundary (MIT engine, AGPL app) is enforced as a topological property of the dependency graph rather than as a policy document. Package selection was measured, not recalled — bundle sizes were built and brotli'd locally, versions and advisories read live from the npm registry — and the selections that contradict received wisdom (jsPDF over pdf-lib, hand-rolled `.ics`, no Turborepo, ESM-only) each have a measurement behind them. That layer needs no further research.

What does need work is everything touching law and truth. Four separate legal errors were inherited from the original brief and have already been corrected in PROJECT.md: Article 6 was cited as Article 7 for the pay-criteria right, a non-existent annual frequency limit on worker requests was asserted (Art. 7(3) is an *employer's* reminder duty), the two-month deadline was presented as the standard when Art. 7(4) makes it a backstop to "a reasonable period of time", and the "fewer than six per sex" suppression threshold was presented as a Directive rule when it is § 12 EntgTranspG — German national law, governing the individual information right rather than Art. 9 reporting, so wrong on both jurisdiction and obligation. These were found in about an hour of diffing the brief against the statute, which tells you the error rate of the vendor and law-firm sources the seed table was built on. Compounding this: **EUR-Lex could not be fetched by any researcher** — three URL forms returned empty 200 responses — so every Directive quotation in this research set reached us through a reader proxy. Re-pulling the primary text is a blocking Phase 0 deliverable, and the empty-200 behaviour is itself a design requirement: the build-time verifier must assert HTTP 200 **plus** a non-empty body above a byte floor **plus** the presence of an expected anchor string, or it will score an empty response as "verified".

The second cross-cutting finding is that **the share-card design as originally specified breaks the zero-egress claim** — Features, Architecture and Pitfalls each reached this independently, by different routes, and Stack added that the Worker does not fit the Cloudflare Free plan's 10 ms CPU limit anyway. A salary in a query string reaches Cloudflare's `ClientRequestURI` log field, the server-side unfurlers at Slack, LinkedIn, Meta and X, every recipient's auto-previewing client, and every human who can simply read the URL in a group chat. Signing the parameters fixes abuse, not disclosure. The convergent resolution is Architecture's: build-time bucketed static cards addressed by coarse public enums (`?c=PL&s=J&b=7`) with the real inputs kept after the `#`, since fragments are never transmitted — which deletes the only server-side component in the project, removes the paid-plan dependency, removes the cost that scales with the viral spike you are engineering for, and yields a *stronger* claim than the original design. PROJECT.md has already adopted this as the default. Treat the remaining risks the same way: the career-gap number must be framed as explicitly conditional arithmetic over a population statistic (never "what the gap cost you"), Module D must refuse to infer worker categories or default any convention the Directive leaves open, and the letter generator must report and assemble but never *evaluate* — the German RDG § 2(1) test turns on legal examination of an individual case, and BGH I ZR 113/20 protects form-driven generators precisely because they do not cross it.

## Key Findings

### Recommended Stack

Every version below was read live from the npm registry on 2026-09-10 and every bundle figure was measured with esbuild plus brotli, not estimated. The one decision that changed the project's premise: **`security.csp` is a stable top-level Astro option only from v6.0.0** (it is `experimental.csp` in 5.x and took a breaking rename *within* the 5.x line), and the zero-egress claim rests on it — so the locked major moved from 5 to 7.3.2. This is not a re-selection of the framework; `@astrojs/react` peer deps are byte-identical across majors, so island code ports unchanged.

**Core technologies:**
- **Astro 7.3.2** (`output: 'static'`, no Cloudflare adapter): site framework — `security.csp` hashes Astro's inline hydration scripts at build time and injects a meta CSP, which is the only way to hold a strict policy without an unsafe-inline allowance
- **TypeScript 7.0.2 + pnpm 12.3.4 workspaces with catalogs**: catalogs are the highest-value monorepo feature here — they stop the engine and the app drifting onto different TS versions, which is how golden vectors start passing locally and failing in CI. No Turborepo (six packages, near-linear graph); add it if CI wall-clock exceeds ~3 minutes
- **`papaparse@5.7.0` + `read-excel-file@9.3.10`** for Module D: 21.5 KB brotli combined, four small transitive packages, no `fetch` anywhere — this pairing *is* the zero-egress claim and it is small enough to audit by hand. **Do not use Papa's worker:true option** (it builds its worker from a `blob:` URL, requiring `worker-src blob:` in the CSP); host Papa inside your own module worker instead
- **`jspdf@4.2.1`** over pdf-lib for client-side PDF: 208 KB brotli with built-in TTF subsetting and `Identity-H`, versus 488 KB for pdf-lib + fontkit with word wrap still unimplemented. Polish diacritics force font embedding either way, since pdf-lib's standard fonts use WinAnsiEncoding and simply throw on the Polish diacritic set
- **`docx@9.7.1`** for DOCX, clean `npm audit`, browser-native. **Correction to the brief: DOCX does not need embedded fonts** — OOXML is UTF-8 XML and rendering uses the reader's system font. The hard font-embedding requirement is PDF-only
- **`@inlang/paraglide-js@2.25.1`** for messages, `astro:i18n` for routing only: Paraglide ships 0 KB of runtime and compiles each message to an exported function, so `.astro` files and React islands use the identical import with no provider or hydration boundary — decisive given 10+ planned locales. Set the locale strategy to URL plus base locale, dropping the cookie strategy, to match the cookieless claim
- **`vitest@4.1.11`** (not the seven-day-old 5.0.0) and **`@playwright/test@1.63.0`**; hand-rolled `.ics` in `packages/letters` rather than `ics@3.12.0`, which drags in `yup` for one VEVENT

**Rejected with cause:** SheetJS (`xlsx`) — the npm copy has been frozen at 0.18.5 since March 2022 carrying two HIGH advisories patched only on SheetJS's own CDN; for a product sold on auditability, a non-npm registry in `.npmrc` is disqualifying. Also rejected: `exceljs` (unmaintained, moderate advisory), `html-docx-js` (HIGH advisories, poor output fidelity), `@react-pdf/renderer` (remote font URLs are its documented happy path), `pdf-lib@1.17.1` (unmaintained since 2021).

**Two open costs:** satori pins `fflate@0.7.3`, inside advisory GHSA-px8p-9vwx-vf98 — override to 0.8.3 or later and verify the render still works. And Cloudflare Pages is not deprecated but lacks features Cloudflare's own docs push you to Workers Static Assets for; worth a deliberate Phase 0 decision either way.

### Expected Features

The employer side is a crowded, demo-gated, enterprise-shaped market (beqom/PayAnalytics, Syndio, Trusaic, Figures, Personio); the worker side is essentially empty. No free browser-only Article 9 calculator was found — the closest free artefact is a UK-metric-shaped Google Sheet. No competitor was found that scores a job ad's salary range. And no vendor publishes machine-readable transposition data: three of them ship free HTML trackers as lead magnets, none ship diffable JSON. That last gap is cheap to fill and disproportionately valuable, because it is the version journalists cite and the community maintains.

**Must have (table stakes):**
- Every legal fact carries a source URL and a per-*fact* `verified_at` — no competitor does this, and even the EU own Your Europe portal shows no last-checked dates
- A "not legal advice" disclaimer that survives export into the PDF, DOCX, `.txt`, `mailto:` body and `.ics` description — it must travel with the artefact, which travels away from the site
- Module A: country selector giving status, legal basis, deadline, anti-retaliation note and equality body; client-side letter with the statutory citation *in* the letter and a computed response date; copy/`.txt`/`.pdf`/`.docx`/`mailto:`; a pending-country mode that is accurate about what *is* in force (in Poland: the recruitment provisions, not the request right)
- Module B: raw-vs-adjusted honesty framing visible **before** the share action and on the card itself — salario.io, Justly and PayAnalytics all already do this, so shipping without it is now *below* the category norm. A copy-link fallback is mandatory, not optional: the Web Share API is not Baseline and is only partial on iOS Safari
- Module D: all seven Art. 9(1) metrics; the 5% Art. 10 flag; **column mapping UI** and a **validation/parse report** (the two most under-specified requirements in the brief, and the ones that decide whether a real HR user gets past their first file); a configurable, documented suppression threshold emitted in the JSON export; privacy flag kept separate from statistical-reliability flag; the quartile tie-breaking rule stated explicitly
- WCAG AA, mobile-first, no signup, no cookie banner

**Should have (competitive):**
- The worker-side letter generator at all — every vendor builds the employer *answer*, nobody builds the worker *question*. This is the whole thesis
- `directive-engine` on npm, MIT, golden vectors: this directly answers the mid-market buying checklist item 5 (reproducible rule-based logic a works council or court can follow) *better than the paid tools do*, because a German works council cannot audit beqom multivariate regression
- All-27 `country-data` as MIT/CC0 machine-readable JSON, with `verified_at` surfaced per fact
- Module C employment-type flag (B2B / umowa zlecenia vs employment contract) — verified and legally load-bearing in Poland, where the December 2025 recruitment provisions reach only prospective *employees*. The strongest single signal and a genuine PL-market differentiator
- The `.ics` reminder — the only zero-egress way to do a reminder; everyone else needs your email

**Defer (v1.x / v2+):**
- **Escalation / follow-up letter generator** when the deadline lapses — datarequests.org shipped exactly this as their v2 headline because first letters get ignored. Highest-value v1.x item, and the best return-visit driver that needs no account
- A "what a compliant Art. 7 answer must contain" checklist per country — the safe form of the tempting "upload your employer response" feature
- One reference national report adapter — **only one national template is even publicly known to exist** (the NL draft, in consultation to 11 Sep 2026). Shipping four adapters at v1 against moving drafts burns a phase and ships four things that will be wrong by January; ship the plugin interface, one reference adapter and a conformance suite instead
- A versioned iframe embed; an official-filings-only employer directory; Art. 10 joint-assessment workflow support

**Sixteen anti-features were catalogued**, of which the dangerous-because-attractive ones are: raw salary in the share URL (A1, existential), employer autocomplete (breaks the same-origin connect-src rule and creates a who-asks-about-whom log), an AI chat assistant (the exact FTC/DoNotPay failure mode), machine-translated letters (a mistranslated statutory citation is invisible to you and worse than nothing), sending the letter on the user behalf, email reminders (an email address is an account by another name), server-side PDF/DOCX rendering (same breach class as A1, harder to spot because it looks like infrastructure), and a live "letters generated" counter (needs an endpoint, needs state, needs the database that is out of scope).

### Architecture Approach

One invariant, stated testably: *for a sentinel value the user typed, no outbound request may contain it in its URL, headers or body, and the set of outbound origins must be a subset of the allowlist.* Everything else is structure in service of that. The build produces 27 x N-locale static pages plus a bounded grid of share-card PNGs; the browser holds all user data; there is no origin logic and, if the bucketed-card recommendation holds, no Worker at all. Legal facts, letters, Eurostat figures and cards are all baked in at build time — **zero runtime data fetch of any kind**.

**Major components:**
1. **`directive-engine`** (MIT, zero runtime deps, zero inbound graph edges) — Art. 9 metrics and the Art. 10 threshold as pure arithmetic over normalised rows, emitting numbers and SCREAMING_SNAKE enum codes only. It must never import `country-data`: the signature takes an options object with `thresholdPct` and `minGroupSizePerSex`, not a country code. Zero inbound edges is what makes the licence boundary a topological property rather than a promise
2. **`country-data`** — one JSON file per country (27 concurrent community PRs against one file is a permanent merge-conflict machine, and per-file gives free CODEOWNERS routing). Every field is a `Fact<T>` envelope, never a bare scalar: value, status, sources, verified_at, with a Zod `superRefine` making a `pending_verification` status force a null value. "Pending verification" therefore becomes unforgeable rather than a UI convention someone forgets. A `directive_default` status distinguishes "the Directive two months applies" from "national law independently says two months" from "we have not checked" — three claims the brief currently collapses into one. Source `kind` tiering lets the UI say "as reported by Littler" instead of "per national law"
3. **`letters`** — templates keyed by (country, language), snapshot-tested to real `.txt` files a native-speaker reviewer can diff in a PR. The resolver must **throw** rather than fall back across countries: a Polish-language letter citing Slovak statutes is silent, plausible-looking and legally wrong
4. **`adapter-kit`** (new package, MIT, zero deps) — the `CountryAdapter` contract plus the conformance suite that gates community PRs. Adapters consume `EngineReport`, never raw payroll rows, so adapter code physically cannot touch personal data and adapter fixtures are safe to commit publicly. Registration is static codegen, never dynamic import — loading unreviewed community code at runtime would require loosening the CSP that is the headline claim
5. **`apps/web`** (AGPL, sink node, depended on by nothing) — Astro site, i18n catalogues, islands, the payroll Web Worker, the CSP, and the egress oracle

**Enforcement mechanisms, strongest first:** the **tarball smoke test** — `npm pack` the engine, extract into a clean temp dir with empty node_modules, compile it and run the golden vectors; if it passes standalone it provably contains no code from elsewhere in the repo. Then a dependency-cruiser graph lint derived from SPDX ids, a zero-deps assertion test, and per-package LICENSE files. Data ships under CC0 separately from code, because a *compilation* of legal facts can attract the EU sui generis database right even though the facts themselves are not copyrightable.

**Where computation runs is a provenance rule, not a size rule:** anything derived from a user-supplied *file* runs in a Web Worker unconditionally — not for performance, but because you cannot know a file size without reading it, and because the Worker is an audit-surface reduction where one reviewable file holds all payroll bytes. Only the few-KB `EngineReport` crosses postMessage; raw rows never return to the scope that has network access. Serving the worker script with its own restrictive CSP converts "the worker does not call fetch" from a code-review claim into a browser-enforced one *(MEDIUM confidence — needs a spike confirming Cloudflare `_headers` applies to a worker script path and that all target browsers enforce it)*. Module B arithmetic, Module C regex and Module A templating all stay on the main thread. Do not add Comlink; the protocol is four messages.

**One honesty correction the architecture forces:** PROJECT.md says Cloudflare Web Analytics means "no third-party origin in the CSP". Stack refines rather than refutes this — with *automatic* injection on a proxied domain the beacon reports to your own domain `/cdn-cgi/rum`, a same-origin POST, so a same-origin connect-src genuinely suffices; the script itself still loads from `static.cloudflareinsights.com` and needs a script-src origin allowance. The README should say "one first-party-operated analytics origin, cookieless, path-only" rather than "no third-party origin", because a hostile reader will open the network tab. Note the CSP gotcha that will otherwise cost an afternoon: when a policy arrives in **both** a header and a meta tag, browsers enforce the **intersection**, so a static `_headers` script-src will block Astro own hashed hydration scripts. Let Astro own script-src and style-src via the meta tag and omit those two directives from `_headers` entirely.

### Critical Pitfalls

1. **Inherited legal errors — four are already in the brief, and the source of the rot is identified.** The seed table sources almost every Directive-level fact to vendor content (mirro.io, papayaglobal.com, deel.com, ey.com), which is written to sell software and copies from other vendor content. This was confirmed live: two vendor pages asserted an FTE-normalisation rule and a six-person suppression rule that appear nowhere in the Directive, and one listed statutory sick pay as includable pay when the Commission FAQ says statutory social-security benefits are not pay (citing *Bilka-Kaufhaus* C-170/84). **Avoid by:** a `source_tier` enum where CI *fails* if a field has only a `secondary` source; vendor pages may be a discovery hint, never a source. Every Directive-level assertion in copy, letters and README carries a cite comment naming article and paragraph. Note the shape of the DoNotPay precedent: what the FTC punished was not wrong law but **unsubstantiated accuracy claims** — and "every legal statement carries a source and a verified date" is exactly that kind of claim, so it must be evidenced.
2. **`verified_at` as decoration rather than control.** Stamped once at seeding, never moved; twelve months later the site displays a stale status with a confident date next to it and a journalist cites it. The date makes the page *look* more trustworthy while making it *less* so. The opposite failure is a blanket 90-day TTL turning the whole dataset red at once until everyone ignores it. **Avoid by:** volatility classes driving per-field TTLs (stable 365 d, volatile 90 d, pending 30 d, statistical until `next_expected_release`); staleness computed at *render* time so the UI degrades automatically; the hard gate firing on the nightly cron and on PRs touching `country-data`, never on an unrelated CSS fix; a content-hash snapshot of each source so a changed hash opens a *review task* rather than auto-updating legal text nobody read; a public `CHANGELOG-legal.md` correction log live on day one with at least one entry, because a log with entries reads as rigour and an empty one reads as new.
3. **The zero-egress claim broken by the project architecture itself** — see the convergent finding above. Beyond the share card and Module C, the leak vectors catalogued are: third-party fonts (LG Muenchen I, 20.01.2022, 3 O 17493/20 awarded 100 EUR for Google Fonts and triggered a mass-Abmahnung wave), analytics beacon URLs, error-reporting SDKs, Referer on outbound links, speculation rules and preconnect, service-worker caches, and any server-side document rendering. **Avoid by:** designing so the sensitive part never appears in a request URL at all; a no-referrer Referrer-Policy plus noreferrer on outbound anchors; a form-action none directive as a machine-checkable expression of the headline claim; a CI grep of the built bundle for network primitives requiring an allowlist annotation per hit; and a `/privacy/proof` page carrying the live CSP header, the allowlist, the CI job link and reproduction instructions — that page, not a README screenshot, is what wins the Hacker News thread.
4. **Getting the Art. 9 metrics wrong in front of the exact audience that can check.** Sixteen specific traps were enumerated against the verbatim Directive text. The load-bearing ones: the denominator is fixed by Art. 3(1) as the **male** average/median, not the overall or midpoint; quartiles are four **equal groups of workers** by count, not four equal pay *ranges*; the Directive is silent on even-count medians, quartile boundary ties and part-time pro-rating, so those must be **explicit caller inputs the engine throws on if missing — never defaults**; "category of workers" is employer-defined under Art. 3(1)(h), so **Module D must require a category column and refuse to guess** (auto-grouping by job title would produce a statutory 5% flag on a category the employer never defined — the single most damaging possible feature); and Art. 9(7) permits self-publication of only (a)-(f) while (g) goes to the authority, so the PDF export needs two modes or it causes a *disclosure* problem for the customer. **Turn the divergence into the asset:** publish a comparison harness running one fixture under the UK convention, the DE convention and the plain-Directive convention, showing three different answers in the README. Also: Art. 9(6) entitles workers representatives to the *methodologies*, so the methodology annex is a feature and a genuine differentiator against vendors who hide the method.
5. **A career-gap number an economist can dismantle in one tweet.** Fourteen criticisms were enumerated with defusals. The strongest defence is that **Eurostat itself** says the unadjusted gap is "broader than discrimination" — quoting the custodian of the statistic is unanswerable. Three hard gates: the Eurostat GPG is **hourly**, so applying it to annual pay assumes identical hours and must say so; it covers enterprises with **10+ employees, NACE B-S excluding O**, so a civil servant or small-firm employee must be told the statistic does not cover them rather than handed a number; 2024 figures are **provisional until December 2026**. Never compound at an investment return, never use male-counterfactual phrasing, never say "cost you" — say "if a gap of X% had applied to your pay for every year you have worked". Design the **negative gap** case first (Luxembourg 2024 is -0.8%). The `/methodology` page ships with the calculator or the calculator does not ship.
6. **Encouraging an irreversible act against the employer without a duty-of-care layer.** The conversion event is a worker sending an identity-revealing letter to their own employer; a tool optimised for "letters generated" is optimised for pushing people over that threshold. Art. 25 protection is *ex post* — a remedy after detriment, not a prevention. **Art. 7(6) is the one nobody puts in a template:** employers may lawfully require that information obtained other than a worker own pay is used only to exercise the equal-pay right, so a worker who posts category averages on LinkedIn may be in breach. **Avoid by:** a non-skippable "before you send" step asking comparison-group size, whether a representative exists, and current employment circumstances — surfacing the identifiability warning and the representative-first route without ever drawing a legal conclusion; a small-employer mode that changes the *default request*; the Art. 7(6) warning shipped inside the artefact; and no "send anonymously" affordance, which would be a false promise and a safety hazard. Germany paragraph 7 RDG carve-out for unions exists for the same reason representative-first routing is the safer product: it is also the easier legal position.
7. **Silent parse failures on real payroll exports.** The engine can be correct and the answer still wrong: a value written as 45 000,00 parses as 45, and the tool prints a confident 22.4% gap that is an artefact of a decimal comma. The catalogued realities are decimal commas with non-breaking-space thousands, mixed conventions *within one file*, Excel date serials and the 1900/1904 epoch, multi-row headers, merged cells, trailing subtotal rows (a phantom worker earning the payroll sum destroys the mean and the top quartile), M/K for the Polish words for man and woman, and CP1250 encoding from older Polish systems. **Avoid by:** treating "fail visibly" as an architecture, not a try/catch — a mandatory mapping-and-confirmation step, a parse report before any metric is shown, a round-trip echo of the first five rows as the engine sees them, an engine that **throws** on non-finite pay rather than coercing, and a **reconciliation checksum** (ask for the total payroll cost, show the parsed total beside it) that catches merged cells, subtotal rows and decimal errors at once and is worth more than any amount of parser cleverness.
8. **Community PRs putting a wrong statute in front of a worker.** A drive-by PR changing a deadline from 2 months to 30 days looks like a typo fix; there is no reputational recovery as cheap as prevention, because the artefact has already been sent. **Avoid by:** a **source-domain allowlist per country** (official journal, parliament, ministry, equality body, EUR-Lex, CURIA) — the single highest-leverage gate; link liveness *plus anchor assertion*; CODEOWNERS and no auto-merge ever; a two-person rule for status and deadline fields; contributions landing in a `proposed/` directory and promoted by a maintainer, so a contributor cannot make the site more confident by merging; and **letters snapshot tests**, so a `country-data` change surfaces in the PR as a diff of the worker-visible letter rather than as a JSON diff. The Public Suffix List is the governance analogue: slow merges are a feature.
9. **The unauthorised-practice boundary, hardest in Germany.** RDG paragraph 2(1) turns on two cumulative elements — a concrete third-party matter *and* a legal examination of the individual case — with paragraph 3 making it a prohibition-with-permission-reservation and paragraph 20 carrying fines. BGH I ZR 113/20 (smartlaw) held a form-driven document generator is not a Rechtsdienstleistung, and paragraph 2(3) no. 5 exempts public discussion of legal questions. **The design rule, enforced in code review: the product may *report* facts and *assemble* the words the user typed; it must never *evaluate*.** Ban any conditional branch whose predicate is a legal characterisation of the user facts. Disclaimers go in four locations (adjacent to the CTA above the fold at 375 px, inside every artefact, on the country page next to `verified_at`, and in the npm README) and must state what the tool *is*, what it explicitly does not do, and who *can* advise — named per country. Poland is the opposite shape: no reserved monopoly on out-of-court advice, but protected titles (never adwokat or radca prawny) and a real unfair-commercial-practices exposure given the Module D to Jarnhaus funnel.
10. **The `.ics` deadline is a legal calculation and it will be wrong.** 31 December plus two months; whether the clock starts on sending or receipt; working days; public holidays. Model it per country as basis, value, starts_from, source_url and verified_at, with starts_from defaulting to unknown; fire the reminder earlier when it is unknown, label it approximate, use all-day floating events, and unit-test 31 Dec, 31 Jan, 29 Feb and leap years.

## Implications for Roadmap

### What is settled and needs no further research

The package set, versions, licences and measured bundle budget. The monorepo shape and the five licence-enforcement mechanisms. The `Fact<T>` envelope and the `resolve()` design. The decision to keep `directive-engine` free of `country-data`. The CSP strategy including the header/meta intersection trap. The client-side export libraries. Paraglide plus `astro:i18n`. The build-time-everything, zero-runtime-fetch posture. The verdict that Module C ships paste-only.

### Phase 0 blockers (nothing downstream is safe until these land)

- **Primary-source re-verification.** Re-pull Directive 2023/970 from EUR-Lex itself — Articles 3(1), 6, 7, 9, 10, 12(3), 25 — and re-verify the entire seed table. Also pull BGH I ZR 113/20 from bundesgerichtshof.de and LG Muenchen I 3 O 17493/20 from dejure or rewis; both reached research only through secondary reporting.
- **The share-card ADR.** PROJECT.md has already adopted build-time bucketed cards as the default; the ADR formalises it, defines the URL parameter contract (which values may appear before the query separator and which must stay after the fragment marker), and records what would have to be true for a dynamic Worker to justify itself.
- **`country-data` governance and CI gates, before the repo is public.** Schema, source-tier rules, source-domain allowlist, the fail-loud verifier (200 plus byte floor plus anchor string), CODEOWNERS, branch protection, the structured PR template, the `proposed/` promotion flow. Test it with a deliberately bad PR.
- **The calculation-convention taxonomy as an ADR.** Denominator, median rule, quartile tie rule, pay basis, partial-period policy, component classification, sex-value mapping, reference period, joiners and leavers. Each is an explicit engine input the engine throws on if missing.
- **Brand name and domain**, the business-model disclosure copy, and the UPL design rule plus disclaimer content spec.

### Suggested phase structure

#### Phase 0: Primary-source verification, data governance, and the decisions that are expensive to change
**Rationale:** Three independent researchers put more into Phase 0 than into any other phase, and for the same reason: the `Fact` envelope is the shape every renderer, template and test consumes, the governance gates must exist before the repo is public, and the four legal errors already found demonstrate the failure mode. Seed all 27 country files immediately with most fields pending verification, so the schema is exercised against reality on day one.
**Delivers:** re-verified Directive text; the `Fact<T>` schema, the shared directive-defaults record, `resolve()`, the Zod-to-JSON-Schema emit and the validator CLI with checks L1-L8; 27 seeded files; the source-tier and source-domain gates; the fail-loud verifier; CODEOWNERS and PR templates; the share-card ADR; the convention taxonomy ADR; the UPL and disclaimer spec; brand and domain.
**Avoids:** Pitfalls 1, 2, 8, 9; sets up 3 and 4.
**Note:** this is a *verification* phase, not a coding phase, and it gates almost everything worker-facing.

#### Phase 0b: Golden vectors and the frozen EngineReport type — data only, no engine code
**Rationale:** The architecture research called this its non-obvious placement, and it holds. The vectors are hand-computed worked examples signed off by a human; they are simultaneously the spec, the test suite, the README audit exhibit and the artefact a compensation analyst reviews. They are cheap to write early and they de-risk the entire B2B door. Freezing `EngineReport` here lets adapter work later proceed in parallel with engine implementation. This is the working agreement in the brief — worked example first — taken literally.
**Delivers:** per-convention vector files, each declaring its own convention set, covering ties across a quartile boundary, headcounts at each remainder mod 4, a one-woman category, an all-male category, a negative gap, zero variable components, and an unmapped sex value; the frozen versioned `EngineReport` type.
**Avoids:** Pitfall 4. **Can start as early as Phase 0** and unblocks the engine, the adapters and Module D simultaneously.

#### Phase 0c: Design system
**Rationale:** The user has decided this lands early and every later phase inherits it rather than re-deciding the visual language. Research independently supports the two hardest items: the **status taxonomy** (in force / from date / draft / pending / pending verification) recurs in the country selector, the country pages, the letter preview and the card, and is expensive to change late; and the share card is a **family** of cards (country x band) that must all read at LinkedIn thumbnail size, so it must be designed and reviewed as a set. Research adds two constraints that invalidate mockups if ignored: legal disclaimers and `verified_at` stamps are legally load-bearing and must be readable rather than shrunk away, and Polish runs 20-30% longer than English.
**Delivers:** tokens, type scale and component language on a **Claude Design canvas** (an Artifact link openable on a phone, since the traffic is mobile); the share-card family and the career-gap result screen as compared artboards; the Article 7 multi-step flow, the pending-country explainer and the **Module D column mapper** proved as throwaway interactive HTML via `/gsd-sketch`, because in those three surfaces behaviour *is* the design and a static mockup would lie; **UI-SPEC.md** as the binding contract underneath both.
**Sequencing:** must follow the share-card ADR (the card family cannot be designed before its parameter contract exists) and should precede the Phase 2 shell.

#### Phase 1: Monorepo skeleton and the zero-egress oracle
**Rationale:** The second non-obvious placement, and PROJECT.md already carries it as a requirement: **write the egress test before there is anything to leak.** A harness that types a sentinel, records every request, and asserts the sentinel appears nowhere while the origin set stays inside the allowlist is trivial against an empty site and near-impossible to retrofit credibly. Its JSON output *is* the README privacy proof — generated on every deploy, not a hand-taken screenshot that rots.
**Delivers:** pnpm workspaces and catalogs; per-package licences; the graph lint; the tarball smoke test; the egress oracle harness and a deliberate-leak test that must go red.
**Uses:** pnpm 12.3.4, Playwright 1.63.0.

#### Phase 2: Web shell and the 27 country pages
**Rationale:** Proves the entire data layer end to end **with no user input at all**, so the first deploy is one where the egress claim is trivially true and the CSP, Lighthouse and Playwright gates get established on easy mode. Also validates the header-versus-meta CSP intersection on a real preview deployment — a one-hour spike now versus a launch-day outage later.
**Delivers:** Astro plus Tailwind plus Paraglide, EN/PL routing and catalogues, Content Layer wiring against the same Zod schema CI uses, the fact-source freshness component, the nightly rebuild cron, and the country pages.
**Implements:** the country-data to UI boundary via `resolve()` only, with a lint forbidding direct value access outside the package.

#### Phase 3: Module B calculator, honesty layer, methodology page, and the bucketed share cards
**Rationale:** First shareable artefact and the Wave 1 driver; independent of the engine, so the B2C launch never waits on Module D. The honesty layer is a **blocking** acceptance criterion, not a polish item, and the methodology page ships with the calculator or the calculator does not ship.
**Delivers:** the calculator on the main thread with fragment-based state; Eurostat data baked in with its own fact provenance and a next-expected-release date; the honesty framing (Marek HITL, plus a hostile pre-mortem as an explicit second gate); the build-time bucketed card grid; the share-capability gate and copy-link fallback.
**Avoids:** Pitfalls 3 and 5.

#### Phase 4: packages/letters, Module A, exports, and the duty-of-care layer
**Rationale:** Gated on the Article 7 and enforcement blocks of `country-data` being *verified* for the launch countries. English-for-any-state falls out of the (country, en) template key for free.
**Delivers:** templates, the multi-step flow, txt/docx/pdf/ics/mailto exports, pending-country mode, the non-skippable before-you-send step, the Art. 7(6) warning in the artefact, and artefact-embedded disclaimers.
**Avoids:** Pitfalls 6, 9, 10.

#### Phase 5: directive-engine implementation and the payroll Worker harness
**Rationale:** Now purely mechanical — the spec from 0b already exists and is executable. TDD against the frozen vectors.
**Delivers:** the engine, the per-path worker CSP, the hand-rolled four-message typed postMessage protocol, the comparison harness for the README.

#### Phase 6: Module D UI, exports, npm publish
**Delivers:** file picker to Worker to parse report to mapping and confirmation to report; the two-mode PDF export (publishable a-f versus all seven for the authority) with the methodology annex; JSON export embedding engine version and every parameter used; the Jarnhaus CTA below the output only; the privacy-proof page; Trusted Publishing to npm with provenance.
**Avoids:** Pitfalls 4 and 7.

#### Phase 7: adapter-kit plus two adapters
**Rationale:** Two adapters, not one — one adapter always fits its own interface; the second is what finds the abstraction bugs. Gated on national templates actually existing.

#### Phase 8: Module C (paste-only)
**Rationale:** Smallest module, safe to sequence late, and the URL-parsing decision must be settled before it starts.

#### Phase 9: Remaining locales and adapters
**Rationale:** Each is now a catalogue plus templates PR touching no engine code — which is the payoff of the boundary discipline. Turn on the Paraglide static-locale flag before the five-locale wave, or the strings for every locale land in one shared bundle.

### Phase Ordering Rationale

- 0 to 1 to 2 is a hard chain; 0b unblocks 5, 6 and 7 simultaneously and should start as early as 0; 0c must follow the share-card ADR and precede the shell.
- Phases 3 and 4 are independent of the engine entirely, so the B2C launch never waits on Module D — which matters because the Polish act publishing in Dziennik Ustaw is the single biggest expected traffic spike and the site must be live before it.
- Two placements are deliberately counter-intuitive and should survive roadmap review: **golden vectors as a Phase 0 data deliverable with no engine code**, and **the egress oracle in Phase 1 before there is anything to leak**.
- Phase 2 delivering a fully static, no-user-input site first means the hardest quality gates (CSP, Lighthouse 100, the egress assertion) are established when they are trivially satisfiable, so later phases inherit a green baseline rather than trying to reach one.

### Gaps to Address

Carried forward as the researchers stated them, without flattening:

- **Directive primary text unverified.** Every Article quotation in this research set is proxy-retrieved or secondary. Handling: **blocking Phase 0.** The verifier built in the same phase must assert 200 plus a non-empty body above a byte floor plus an expected anchor string, because empty 200s were *observed* and a naive checker would score them as verified.
- **Module C job-ad URL parsing is unresolved and may be incompatible with the zero-egress claim outright.** No job portal sends permissive CORS, so client-side fetching is impossible; the alternatives are a server-side proxy (forbidden by the architecture), a bookmarklet, or a browser extension (which moves user data into a context where the egress proof no longer holds, plus two store review processes and a DOM-change maintenance treadmill). **Recommendation: paste-only for v1**, with the URL field dropped rather than left as a broken affordance. Handling: **must be decided before Phase 8**; paste-only is the working assumption until then.
- **National small-group and suppression thresholds outside Germany** (IE, AT, SE, FR, LT, NL) are unverified. Handling: **needed before the Phase 7 adapters.** Default the minimum group size to null; DE is 6 only via paragraph 12 EntgTranspG and only for that obligation.
- **Whether any Member State has taken the Art. 12(3) restricted-access option, and which.** This changes what a worker should even *ask for*, so it is a per-country `country-data` field. Handling: Phase 0 seeding, Phase 4 consumption.
- **The two judgments** (BGH I ZR 113/20 on the smartlaw generator; LG Muenchen I 3 O 17493/20 on Google Fonts) reached research only through secondary reporting. Handling: pull the texts in Phase 0 before either is cited publicly.
- **The Polish UPL position** (no reserved monopoly on out-of-court advice; protected titles; court representation restricted under art. 87 k.p.c.) came from search-derived commentary. Handling: needs a Polish-qualified check.
- **No quantitative retaliation-incidence data for EU pay enquiries exists in what was searched.** The available literature is US pay-secrecy research documenting prevalence, not retaliation rates. Handling: **do not cite a retaliation statistic.** The honest UI line is qualitative.
- **The representative-first pattern in union and NGO tooling** is an inference, not a verified inventory. Handling: treat as a design hypothesis and validate with two or three unions before launch — which doubles as the pre-briefing that immunises against the fearmongering charge.
- **National reporting templates barely exist publicly.** Only the NL draft (consultation to 11 Sep 2026) is known. Handling: ship the interface and one reference adapter; do not commit to four.
- **Legacy binary .xls is unsupported** by read-excel-file, and old HR systems do export it. Handling: detect the magic bytes and tell the user to re-save; do **not** pull in SheetJS to cover it.
- **Eurostat 2024 figures are provisional until December 2026.** Handling: store a next-expected-release date, print "provisional" and the reference year on the card itself, and set a calendar task.
